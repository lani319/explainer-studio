- In nine articles, it sets out the basic framework of the state before anything else.

- First, a quick look at the whole chapter.
- It defines the form of the state and its sovereignty, its nationals and its territory,
- then turns to unification and peace, international law and the status of foreigners.
- It closes with public officials, political parties, and the State's efforts for culture.

## Article 1 · A democratic republic {#art1}

```screen
type: quote
kicker: Article 1
title: Democratic republic, popular sovereignty
note: Unofficial translation
items:
  - {label: ①, text: "The Republic of Korea is a **democratic republic**."}
  - {label: ②, text: "The sovereignty of the Republic of Korea **resides in the people**, and all state power comes from the people."}
points: [Democratic republic, Popular sovereignty]
```

- Paragraph one declares that Korea is a **democratic republic**.
- Paragraph two says that sovereignty **resides in the people**, and that all state power comes from the people.
- So the very first article names both the form of the state and who holds sovereignty.

## Articles 2 and 3 · Nationals and territory {#art2-3}

```screen
type: quote
kicker: Articles 2 · 3
title: Nationals and territory
note: Unofficial translation
items:
  - {label: 2-①, text: "The requirements for becoming a national of the Republic of Korea are **determined by law**."}
  - {label: 2-②, text: "As provided by law, the State has a duty to **protect nationals living abroad**."}
  - {label: "3", text: "The **territory** of the Republic of Korea consists of the Korean Peninsula and its adjacent islands."}
```

- Article 2 leaves the question of who becomes a **national** to be **determined by law**.
- It also gives the State a **duty to protect nationals living abroad**, as provided by law.
- Article 3 defines the **territory** as the Korean Peninsula and its adjacent islands.

```screen

  - {text: "The Republic of Korea **seeks unification**, and formulates and carries out a **policy of peaceful unification** based on the free and democratic basic order."}
```

- Article 4 states that Korea **seeks unification**.
- The means is a **policy of peaceful unification**, based on the free and democratic basic order.

## Article 5 · Peace and the armed forces {#art5}

```screen
type: quote
kicker: Article 5
title: International peace and the armed forces
note: Unofficial translation
items:
  - {label: ①, text: "The Republic of Korea endeavors to maintain international peace and **renounces wars of aggression**."}
  - {label: ②, text: "The armed forces have the mission of national security and defense of the land, and their **political neutrality** is upheld."}
```

- Under paragraph one, Korea works to maintain international peace and **renounces wars of aggression**.
- Paragraph two sets the mission of the armed forces as security and defense, and upholds their **political neutrality**.


- Paragraph two: the status of foreigners is guaranteed as provided by **international law and treaties**.


- Public officials are **servants of the people as a whole**, and they are responsible to the people.

## Article 8 · Political parties {#art8}

```screen
type: flow
title: "Article 8 — four paragraphs on political parties"
steps:
  - {title: Free to establish, sub: "① Parties may be freely founded; multi-party system guaranteed", at: 0}
  - {title: Democratic parties, sub: "② Aims, organization and activities must be democratic", at: 1}
  - {title: Protection by the State, sub: "③ Protected by law; the State may subsidize operating funds", at: 2}
  - {title: Dissolution, sub: "④ Government petitions → Constitutional Court decides", at: 3}
```

- Article 8 covers parties. First, they may be founded **freely**, and multiple parties are guaranteed.
- Second, a party's aims, organization and activities must be **democratic**.
- Third, the State protects parties as provided by law, and may **subsidize** their operating funds.
- Fourth, if a party's aims or activities violate the basic democratic order,
- the Government may petition the **Constitutional Court** to dissolve it.
- The party is then dissolved by the Court's **decision**.
